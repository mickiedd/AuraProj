# 2026-09-22

## Guangzhou landmark showcase — one independent light per building

User: "in the GuangzhouLandmark shoucase level, each building should have a independent
light." Two choices put to the user first, both answered: the light is an **accent added
on top of** the unchanged global key (7.0) / fill (2.0) / sky (1.4), and it is a
**spot light** so the cone can be confined.

### What was built

`Scripts/CreateGuangzhouLandmarkShowcase.py` gained `spawn_landmark_lights(entries)`:
one shadow-casting `SpotLight` per landmark, standing in front of its own building on the
plaza side and above its roof, aimed at the middle of the building, **attached to the
landmark actor** (`KEEP_WORLD` on all three rules), labelled `Light_<key>`, tagged
`GuangzhouLandmarkLight`, folder `LandmarkLights`. Level went 25 → 34 actors.

Every parameter is a ratio of the building's own measured geometry, so a swapped-in
variant stays correctly lit: facade clearance `radius + 1500 cm`; height `1.35 × h`; aim
at `0.45 × h`; outer cone = subtended half-angle + 10° (max 80°); inner = `0.45 × outer`;
attenuation `1.15 × (distance + radius)`; intensity `3.0 lux × distance_m²` candelas.
Intensity is computed rather than fixed because candelas are not scale-free — a constant
value would light a 15 m gate and a 30 m gate very differently.

`clear_previous()` needed a change: destroying a landmark destroys its attached light, so
the actor snapshot holds actors that may already be gone. Per-actor steps are now guarded
and the level is **asserted** clear afterwards (the assertion, not the count, proves it).

### The measurement that mattered

Isolation was originally going to be measured centre-to-centre, which would have reported
24° of clearance. The honest test is against the neighbour's whole **body**: a gate 70°
off the axis still subtends ~20° from this light. Real worst-case clearance is **9.81°**
(Guidemen's light vs Wuxianmen V5 4K Core). Both the builder and the validator now use
`angle − neighbour_span − cone`.

### Validation

`Scripts/ValidateGuangzhouLandmarkShowcase.py`, reloaded from disk: **0 errors, 0
warnings**, 34 actors. Each light is **re-derived** from the placement manifest's own
recipe plus the landmark's recorded geometry rather than compared against the numbers the
builder wrote about itself: placement error 0.0 cm, aim error 0.0° on all nine, settings
within 1e-4 relative, isolation 0 violations. Ring / grounding / overlap checks still pass.

### Measured effect

`Scripts/CaptureGuangzhouLandmarkLights.py` — every light's intensity zeroed, then
restored, level never saved, all nine intensities verified back on disk. Guidemen view
(eye level, 56 m): mean **0.638 → 0.712**; the gate goes from a flat black silhouette to a
lit facade with masonry, red columns and roof. Plaza +0.017, hero +0.002.

### Capture-harness defect found and fixed (the surprise of the session)

The bracket frames came back clipped, and chasing why found a real defect:

1. The exposure pin **did not reach the capture at all** — EV100 3.0/4.0/5.0/6.0 produced
   frames with identical statistics (mean 222.51 / 240.99, clipped 78.91% / 93.07%). An 8×
   exposure change must change the image.
2. Two of the three flags the capture script set — `b_always_persist_rendering_state`,
   `b_capture_on_construction` — **do not exist** on this build's
   `SceneCaptureComponent2D` (real name `always_persist_rendering_state`, no `b_`), and
   `try/except` swallowed the failure so the script reported a configuration it never
   applied.
3. **`capture_every_frame = False` is the culprit.** Default flags separate EV100 3.0/7.0
   by 60× (means 1440.5 vs 23.9); `always_persist_rendering_state = False` separates them
   by nothing (17831.4 vs 17831.5).
4. The **archived 2026-09-21 bracket was sound** — `BracketShowcaseExposure.py` never set
   those flags and its frames do respond (215.13 / 199.96 / 85.32 / 24.80). So the level's
   EV100 3.0 pin rests on a working bracket; the showcase captures taken since did not.

Fixed in `CaptureGuangzhouLandmarkShowcase.py` and `BracketShowcaseLandmarkLight.py`.
`CaptureGuangzhouLandmarkLights.py` now **verifies exposure control before trusting any
frame** (measured EV100 3.0 → 0.838, 6.0 → 0.094) and fails loudly otherwise.

### Follow-up, same session — both open items resolved, one of my own claims corrected

**I was wrong about the mid-tones.** The "no exposure yields mid-tones" reading came from
the broken-harness bracket, whose four frames were all the *same* uncontrolled exposure.
With the harness fixed, EV100 4.0 puts **30% of pixels in the mid-tones** and the scene is
legible. I had written that wrong claim into the record and the memory, and corrected both.

**The blue lower half is the ground plane, and it is fine.** Ray traces
(`Scripts/ProbeShowcaseFrameGeometry.py`) hit `Showcase_Ground` at 90 m / 28 m / 15 m for
rows 560 / 700 / 860 of the eye-level frame, and straight down. It is dark paving lit cool
by the sky light; the saturated cyan at EV100 3.0 was the red channel clipping first on a
cool surface, not a different surface.

**One real composition finding:** row 510 (pitch −0.5°) hits *nothing*. That camera sits
50 m from the plaza centre and the 420 m ground plane ends 160 m away, so the ray leaves
the plane's footprint before reaching the ground — that band of the frame is the
SkyAtmosphere below the horizon. Both are blue, so the plane's edge is invisible and the
blue reads as continuous. Enlarging the ground plane is worth doing if the level is ever
viewed on foot.

**The legacy capture scripts are moot, not broken-in-a-new-way.** `CaptureDadongmen*.py`
(5) and `CaptureZhengnanmenManual4K.py` do set `capture_every_frame = False`, but all six
write their images with `export_render_target`, which writes nothing in this build. They
produce no output at all, so the flag is irrelevant there — deliberately left alone rather
than "fixed" into looking functional.

Also fixed a wrong index in my own probe: `HitResult.to_tuple()[8]` is the PhysicalMaterial,
not the actor (9 is the actor, 4 is the location), and `line_trace_single` returns `None`
rather than an empty result on a miss. Both now recorded in the project memory.

### Follow-up 3 — the ground plane, 420 m → 8000 m

Acted on the one finding left open. `GROUND_SCALE` 420 → 8000 (half extent 210 m →
4000 m; ground bounds ±21000 → ±400000 cm). Sized by measurement, not taste: the band is
`atan(camera height / half extent)` tall and 25 px per degree at 900 px over a 36° vertical
FOV, so 210 m gives 27 px, 1000 m gives 6 px, 4000 m gives 1 px — 8000 m is the first size
that both removes the band and pushes the edge out of the hero frame (4.2°, i.e. out of a
frame spanning −13.7° to −49.7°). One quad with a textureless constant material, so the
size costs nothing. Ring, landmark positions, the nine lights (59–90 m attenuation),
labels and exposure pin all unchanged.

**Also corrected a real bug in my own capture script.** When no bracket step met the
clipping target, `CaptureGuangzhouLandmarkLights.py` fell back to the *least clipped*
frame — which is the darkest step, the opposite of useful. It now falls back to the frame
with the **most mid-tones**, which on this level selects EV100 4.0 (23.1% mid-tones) over
5.0 (3.4%) and 6.0 (0.0%).

Re-validated: builder `passed: true`, validator **0 errors, 0 warnings**, 34 actors,
9 landmark lights, isolation 0 violations. Re-captured at EV100 4.0: hero view is ground to
all four edges with no void, eye-level band is a single pixel.

### Follow-up 4 — my "weakest two" claim was wrong; the lights are uniform within 1.17×

I had recorded that the two Wuxianmen lights were the weakest pair "at roughly a tenth of the
strongest" and recommended strengthening them. **That was wrong, and the metric was the
fault.** A whole-frame mean delta is dominated by how much of the picture the building
fills, and Zhengnanmen HighFidelity (2302 cm tall) fills far more of its frame than
Wuxianmen FullPBR (1721 cm).

`Scripts/AnalyzeLandmarkLightContribution.py` recomputes the same frames as the mean
brightening **on the pixels the light actually brightens**:

| building | frame Δ | coverage | Δ on lit px |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GreatNorthGate | 0.0328 | 20.1% | **0.7124** |
| Guidemen V5 4K | 0.0411 | 22.8% | 0.6837 |
| Wuxianmen V5 FullPBR | 0.0079 | 18.8% | 0.6825 |
| Wuxianmen V5 4K Core | 0.0123 | 20.1% | 0.6670 |
| Xiaobeimen Production V3 | 0.0326 | 23.7% | 0.6602 |
| Zhenhai Tower | 0.0441 | 24.2% | 0.6470 |
| Xiaobeimen AAA V3 | 0.0845 | 31.4% | 0.6445 |
| Zhengnanmen HighFidelity | 0.1054 | 30.9% | 0.6177 |
| Zhengnanmen AAA V3 | 0.0626 | 27.6% | **0.6111** |

The nine lights land within **1.17×** of each other and the ordering **reverses** — the two
Wuxianmen lights are third and fourth *strongest*, while Zhengnanmen, with by far the
largest frame delta, is weakest. The 1.67× coverage spread is the framing effect and nothing
else. **So there is nothing to tune**, and the uniformity is the recipe working as designed:
`intensity = 3.0 lux × distance²` gives every building the same illuminance from its own
light whatever its size, which a fixed candela value would not.

**Lesson worth keeping: never compare lights on differently sized subjects with a
whole-frame mean.** The contact sheet now annotates the fair figure, and the record, the
index and this log all carry the correction.
