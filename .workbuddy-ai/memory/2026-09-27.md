# 2026-09-27

## Xiaobeimen AAA V3 — three fixes from one report

User, from a viewport screenshot plus the reference board: "the bricks of the walls need to be
optimized, and also the plaque's position is not correct, the wooden door also need to fit the
arc perfectly."

`BP_Xiaobeimen_AAA_V3` is the **surviving** Xiaobeimen landmark (`Xiaobeimen_Production_V3` was
retired 2026-09-22), so it was the right target — worth checking before touching a retired asset.

### The shape of the job was different from Zhengdongmen

No procedural source exists. It is a V3 Blueprint of **104 subobjects** whose meshes are binary
`.uasset`. So the first move was to **read the geometry out of the engine**:

- `StaticMeshDescription` is **per-element** — `get_vertex_position`,
  `get_triangle_vertex_instances`, `get_vertex_instance_uv`. There is no `get_vertex_positions`,
  and `VertexID` exposes no id accessor in this build, so the dump records positions and
  per-triangle UVs and skips indices.
- `AssetTools.export_assets` takes **two** arguments and produced FBX, not OBJ. The
  `unreal.TextureExporterPNG` class exists but `export_asset_tasks` does not — texture export was
  abandoned in favour of reading the mesh.
- Measured: components carry a **-90 roll**; mesh-local is `(actor_x, actor_z, -actor_y)`;
  arch **springing z 370, intrados r 265, crown z 635**, extrados 315; wall UVs **80 cm per
  unit** on all twelve faces; door leaves 146.6 cm wide with flat tops at z 344.5.

### The three fixes

1. **Wall** — autocorrelating a native capture gave stone units of **18 × 15 cm** against the
   reference's ~50 × 28. Fixed by authoring a seamless ashlar set (11 courses × 6 blocks,
   53 × 29 cm mean, 2 cm mortar, joints recessed in the height map) **and** inserting
   `TexCoord -> Multiply(0.25)` into `M_StoneWall` so one map covers 320 cm.
2. **Plaque** — it was *inside* the arch opening; raised **+156 cm** to sit on the wall above the
   crown, centred in the 152.5 cm between crown and coping.
3. **Door** — rebuilt from a new source generator as two closed leaves whose top edge is the
   intrados, plus a stud grid following the arc.

### Four traps, all new

- **glTF positions are metres.** A file carrying centimetres lands **100× too large** without
  `import_offset_uniform_scale = 0.01`. The first import attempt produced a 26,400 cm door.
- **Interchange's roll mapping has to be measured, not reasoned.** At **roll 0** it maps the
  file's `(X, Y, Z)` to mesh-local `(X, Z, Y)`. Reasoning from the Zhengdongmen convention
  (`(x, -y, z)`) gave the wrong answer twice; the fix script now tries all four rolls and keeps
  the one whose box matches.
- **A material with no parameters still has a graph you can edit.** `M_StoneWall` is five
  TextureSample nodes wired straight to their properties. `get_material_expressions` does **not**
  exist, but `get_material_property_input_node` does, so the graph can be walked from its
  outputs. Adding a TexCoord pair is **not idempotent**, so the fix report is the record that
  decides whether to re-apply it.
- **Chaining a commandlet and a GUI editor in one shell command is not reliable.** A chained
  `fix; capture` run silently skipped the fix — the report was stale and the "after" capture came
  out byte-identical to the "before". Run them separately and check the report's mtime.

### Also worth keeping

- **A first texture pass failed the other way.** Too much weathering noise swamped the coursing
  and the wall read as flat camouflage — worse than the fine brick it replaced. The amplitudes
  are now named constants at the top of the script with that recorded.
- **The door's pose is a judgement call, and it went against the board.** The reference photo
  shows this gate open; the same phrasing on Zhengximen ("fit the arc perfectly") wanted closed
  leaves filling the arch, so closed is what this does, superseding the 2026-09-14 "±240 cm, 65°
  open" alignment. Flagged in the report so the user can redirect.
- The leaves meet with **no centre gap**: any daylight between them shows the sky through the
  unmodelled tunnel.

### Verification

`Scripts/FixXiaobeimenAAA.py` re-run end to end: **passed, no problems**. Both door meshes land in
their expected mesh-local boxes at roll 0; the plaque reports `changed: false` on the second run.
Six native captures before and after, cleanup 0 → 0.

### Open

- The wall's 320 cm repeat is still visible on a 30 m wall.
- The pre-existing `Stone_SurfaceDetail` and vegetation meshes read as **dark holes** on the wall —

- No knocker (铺首) on the new leaves.
- `M_StoneWall`'s UV scale is now global to every part that samples it, including the ramp.

## Follow-up: the 锯齿 on the arch — it was not the door

User: "there're 锯齿 in that arc, try to increase the vertices of the arc to fix this issues."

**The arc they were looking at was not the one I controlled.** Measured every arc in the model
before touching anything:

| arc | tessellation | chord |
|---|---|---|
| door leaves (I authored them) | 1.0 cm sampling, 527 columns | 1.0 cm |
| voussoir ring `Arch_Voussoirs` | 6° | 27.75 cm |
| **wall's arch curve `Wall_ArchSpandrel`** | **~4°, 38 angle samples** | **~18 cm** |

A 1 cm chord on a 265 cm radius deviates from the true arc by **0.0005 cm** — a thousandth of a
pixel at 0.63 cm/px. So refining the door would have changed *nothing*, and the instinct "increase
the vertices of the arc" pointed at the right idea but the wrong mesh. The stepping was the
shading of the **wall's arch cut**.

**The general rule: when a report is about a curve's smoothness, measure the tessellation of every
curve in the model before refining the one you own.** The coarsest arc is usually in a part you
did not author. And do the arithmetic — `sagitta = R(1 − cos(Δθ/2))` — because a chord that looks
big can be sub-pixel and a chord that looks small can be the whole problem.

### The fix

`ContentSource/GuangzhouLandmarks/Xiaobeimen/source/build_xiaobeimen_arch_spandrel.py` rebuilds
`Wall_ArchSpandrel` at **0.5° / a 2.31 cm chord** (1,626 tris vs the original 432), plus the door
refined from 1.0 to 0.25 cm as asked.

Its shape came from reconstructing the original mesh — possible because the mesh is **fully
unwelded** (1296 verts for 432 tris), so triangle *t* uses positions 3t..3t+2 and the topology is
readable from a dump that only records positions and per-triangle UVs. That is a useful trick: it
means skipping `VertexID` accessors costs nothing.

The panel is `{(x, z) : |x| <= 260, z_arc(x) <= z <= 630.2}` with `z_arc(x) = 370 + sqrt(265² − x²)`.
That region is empty where the arc rises above the panel's top — which is exactly what makes it
**two** spandrel panels rather than one, so the generator samples the arc by angle and drops the
points above the top rather than special-casing the split.

### Verified

`FixXiaobeimenAAA.py` re-run: **passed, no problems**; all three meshes land in their expected
mesh-local boxes at roll 0. The staircase is gone at pixel scale.

### Open, and now more visible

With the arc smooth, the **dark rectangular patches and dark spheres** on the wall stand out more.
They are the pre-existing `Stone_SurfaceDetail` and vegetation meshes and read as holes punched
through the wall. Flagged to the user; not part of this ask.
