# Elevation sources

`ELEV-002` and `ELEV-003` are window crops of [OpenGeoHub GEDTM30 v1.1](https://zenodo.org/records/15689805), under CC BY 4.0. `GEDTM30_Guangzhou_context_20250611.tif` is a modeled **modern bare-earth DTM** and `GEDTM30_Guangzhou_uncertainty_20250611.tif` is the corresponding uncertainty field. Both are 720 × 520 pixels at 0.00025° per pixel, covering 113.19–113.37°E and 23.07–23.20°N without resampling. The vertical reference is EGM2008 / EPSG:3855. Honor raster scale/offset metadata: elevation scale 0.1 m per stored unit; uncertainty 0.01 m per stored unit. The crop ranges about −14.4 to 361.8 m elevation and 0.08 to 11.91 m uncertainty where defined.

The 2006–2015-era machine-learning estimate supports **modern regional context only**. Urban fill, cut, demolition and river engineering prevent direct use as 1880–1900 ground height. `ELEV-001` Copernicus GLO-30 is a surface model with buildings and vegetation, so it was not used as the bare-earth source.

The official [Guangta Road/Chashu Lane excavation notice](https://wglj.gz.gov.cn/xxgk/bmwj/ywxx/wwl/content/post_10742941.html) links a 177-page report (`ARCH-003`). It gives relative depths but no identified absolute late-Qing ground level. A research copy is in ignored `Saved/TerrainTools/`; the register links the official PDF.

A [2021 stratigraphy paper](https://doi.org/10.13284/j.cnki.rddl.003304) by Sun Yat-sen University and Guangzhou's archaeology institute (`ARCH-004`) reports a **Qing L3 cultural layer at 5.92–6.22 m sea-level elevation** at the Jiefang Middle Road site. The paper gives site coordinates, but does not state the specific vertical datum realization or identify the layer as an 1880 walking surface. `Data/Elevation_Constraints.csv` records the published range as `candidate_stratum_not_terrain`, with no point height. It is excluded from historical terrain interpolation pending datum reconciliation and archaeological interpretation.
